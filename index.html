Dojo etiquette · 礼儀

How we carry ourselves

Judo begins and ends with respect. New to the dojo? These five habits cover almost everything.

礼

Bow — and mean it

Bow when entering or leaving the dojo, every time you step on or off the tatami — on with the left foot, off with the right — and before and after every partner drill or randori. A standing bow (ritsurei) is a straight-backed 30°, never rushed.

着

Respect the gi

Wash your judogi after every session, keep it free of rips, and tie your belt properly. A cared-for uniform shows consideration for every partner who grips it.

清

Arrive early, arrive clean

Be changed and ready before class starts. Nails trimmed short, long hair tied back with a soft band, all jewelry off — rings, necklaces, earrings, watches. Your partners' safety depends on it.

畳

Honor the mat

No shoes, phones, food, or drinks on the tatami — and no bare feet off it. Sit in seiza or cross-legged when not training. Running late? Wait at the mat's edge, raise your hand, and join when Sensei invites you on.

師

Sensei & Senpai

Address the instructor as Sensei and senior students with courtesy. Watch how the senior grades conduct themselves — that's the fastest way to learn the culture.

道

It's a path, not a rulebook

Kanō Jigorō taught judo as a dō — a path of physical, intellectual, and moral growth. Pick one etiquette habit each session, practice it deliberately, and carry it beyond the dojo.

How every class opens and closes

Highest rank on the right, lowest on the left. When the class kneels in seiza or rises, wait for the senior grades to move first.

Anatomy of a throw

  1. 1
    Kuzushi 崩しBreak uke's balance and posture — move them, then control them.
  2. 2
    Tsukuri 作りBuild the technique: fit your body into position.
  3. 3
    Kake 掛けExecute — lift, pull, or push, and steer the fall.
  4. 4
    Kime 極めFollow through, in control to the very end.

How a match is scored

  • Ippon 一本Full point — the match is over.
  • Waza-ari 技ありHalf point — two make an ippon.
  • Yuko 有効Smallest score — never adds up to a waza-ari.

Four ways to score

  • ThrowUke lands on the back, with force, speed & control
  • Pin5 s yuko · 10 s waza-ari · 20 s ippon
  • ChokeUke taps to submit
  • ArmlockUke taps to submit

Belt ranks · 段級位

The road from white to red

Judo progresses through the kyu (student) grades, then ten dan (black-belt) grades. Every belt on this wall is earned on the mat — the last two are earned over a lifetime.

Kyu 級 · student grades

  1. WhiteRokkyu · 6th kyu
  2. YellowGokyu · 5th kyu
  3. OrangeYonkyu · 4th kyu
  4. GreenSankyu · 3rd kyu
  5. BrownIkkyu · 1st kyu

Dan 段 · black-belt grades

  1. BlackShodan – Sandan · 1st – 3rd dan
  2. Black & RedYondan – Godan · 4th – 5th dan
  3. Red & WhiteRokudan – Hachidan · 6th – 8th dan
  4. RedKudan – Judan · 9th – 10th dan

About CAJA

A club built on maximum efficiency, mutual welfare

Carolina's American Judo Association sits in Matthews, North Carolina — between the two Carolinas we serve. Our mission is simple: educate, inspire, and connect judoka throughout the Carolinas, whether they step on the mat to compete, to get fit, or to find a second family.

“Maximum efficiency with minimum effort. Mutual welfare and benefit.” — Kanō Jigorō, founder of Kodokan Judo · 精力善用 · 自他共栄
